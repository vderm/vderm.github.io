## About this project catalogue

Here is, in alphabetical order, a collection of some of my very past work. I've built other things during this period but they've failed so hard that I rather just forget about them! Most of the software files are no longer functional due to outdated libraries and since AI coding is so good that it's probably easier to just ask Claude to build it again, that I decided not to include them. If you're really interested, contact me.

The main themes are: woodworking, 3d-printing often involving some form of parametric design using Rhino [Grasshopper](https://www.rhino3d.com/features/#grasshopper), electronics often involving the Arduino or ESP32, and drawing machines often involving a form of CNC.

## bicycle wall stand

*Tags: woodworking, design, drawing*

Bicycle stand stuck to my wall, for my bicycles.

Two main designs. One is used for my Giant (top) which is pretty much a small
support for each tire that's a piece of wood with two wooden dowels. The bike is
tilted and held in place with strings connecting to an eye screw secured to a
stud in the wall. The other support is a more common one that's a shelf with a
slot for the bike to slide into it. My wood piece wasn't too large so I spaced
out the shelf part to have stripes of wood. The drawings show the calculations
in order for any part of the bikes to not interfere with the wall.

## cnc cd version

*Tags: electronics*

I made a pen plotter CNC machine with old drives.

This was my first CNC machine. I salvaged the electronics/mechanisms from a CD
drive and two floppy (diskette) drives. The steppers have 4 wires. You can use a
multi-meter to determine which pair goes with which. I've added old switches as
limit switches. The design, although simple, took me weeks to come up with. A
small piece of wood is used to hold the two bottom stepper motor modules
together (X & Y axes). The Z axis just goes up and down. I had to put a piece of
wood to push the Z axis forward so that the pen can effectively use most of the
platform.

## cnc spindle mount

*Tags: design*

Cheap Chinese CNC spindle mount broke, designed a replacement.

This was a piss-off. Bought my CNC from aliexpress.com. The spindle mount was 3D
printed! Not only that, it was printed using the fastest settings. It was not
structurally stiff and I had to tighten the screws otherwise the spindle would
move. Over time, the mount fractured. I asked the company to send me the *.stl
file but they didn't. So I took some measurements and made my own. The location
of the back hole (where the Z-axis screw connects) is 1 mm out of plane and
should be moved away from the spindle.

I ended up not really using this CNC machine much. Too flimsy.

CNC model: 2417 from aliexpress.com

## espresso machine table

*Tags: design, coffee*

Granite top metal leg espresso machine table. Class.

When I finally decided to buy the Profitec 700 espresso machine, I couldn't just
have it sit anywhere. It demanded a pedestal. Something of class and beauty. My
brother got me a slab of marble 20x36 inches and I needed to design the legs.
Initially, I wanted to have drawers, but the result wasn't as good and my
woodworking skills aren't great. I measured the height where the machine should
be and went on from there. I was afraid that the weight of the granite and
machine would push the legs out so I added bracing. They're kind of a nice
touch.
